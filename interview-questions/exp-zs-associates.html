02Questions and model answers

Q1
What is the difference between linear and logistic regression?
Linear regression Logistic regression
Target Continuous Binary (or multi-class with softmax)
Output ŷ = wᵀx + b, unbounded p = σ(wᵀx + b) in (0, 1)
Loss MSE (least squares) Log-loss (cross-entropy)
Interpretation Coefficient = change in y Coefficient = change in log-odds (exp → odds ratio)
Assumptions Linearity, homoscedastic errors Linear in the log-odds; independent observations
Q2
Why is ReLU preferred in neural networks?
  • No saturation for positive inputs: the gradient is 1, which avoids the vanishing gradients of sigmoid and tanh, so deep networks train faster.
  • Cheap to compute (max(0, x)), and produces sparse activations.
  • Downside: "dying ReLU" (units stuck at 0). Fixes are Leaky ReLU, PReLU, ELU and GELU/SiLU (used in Transformers), plus a good initialisation (He) and learning rate.
Q3
Precision vs recall: when do you prioritise each? (Give a pharma example.)
  • Precision: when false positives are costly. For example, flagging physicians for an expensive field-sales visit means we want flagged doctors to really be high-potential.
  • Recall: when misses are costly. For example, detecting adverse-event mentions in medical text, where missing one is a compliance risk.
  • Balance with F1 or a cost-weighted threshold, and show the precision-recall curve to stakeholders so they can choose the operating point.
Q4
How do you evaluate a RAG application?
  • Retrieval: recall@k, MRR, context precision (are the retrieved chunks relevant?).
  • Generation: faithfulness (claims supported by the context), answer relevance, correctness against ground truth.
  • Tools: RAGAS, DeepEval, TruLens, LangSmith evaluations; a golden set of 100+ questions written with domain experts.
  • Online: user feedback, citation click-through, escalation rate, latency and cost.
  • Regression testing: re-run the suite on every prompt, model or index change.
Q5
What is MLflow and how do you use it?
  • An open-source platform for the ML lifecycle:
  • Tracking: log parameters, metrics and artefacts per run, and compare experiments.
  • Projects: reproducible runs.
  • Models: a standard packaging format, served via REST.
  • Model Registry: versions plus stages (staging/production), approvals and lineage.
  • Usage: mlflow.autolog() in training, register the best model, and deploy from the registry in CI/CD. Newer versions also trace and evaluate LLM apps.
Q6
How do you treat outliers in a modelling dataset?
  • Investigate first: an error (fix or remove) or a real extreme (keep it; it may be important).
  • Detect: IQR rule, z-score (on roughly normal data), visual checks, Isolation Forest for multivariate outliers.
  • Treat: cap/winsorise, log transform, robust scalers or losses (Huber), tree-based models.
  • Document the impact on metrics, and never "clean away" the signal you're trying to predict.
Q7
Guesstimate: how many diabetes patients visit endocrinologists in Bengaluru each month?

Answer (show your structure):

  • Bengaluru population about 1.4 crore → adults about 70% ≈ 98 lakh.
  • Diabetes prevalence in urban adults about 12% → about 11.8 lakh diabetics; about 60% diagnosed ≈ 7 lakh.
  • Share seeing an endocrinologist (rather than a GP) about 20% ≈ 1.4 lakh patients; visits every 3 months → about 47,000 visits a month.
  • State your assumptions and sanity-check them: about 300 endocrinologists × about 160 visits a month ≈ 48,000. That matches.

03How to prepare for ZS

  • Practise a 2–3 hour classification case: clean, model, evaluate, then present your choices clearly.
  • Revise ML basics and SQL; prepare puzzles and guesstimates.
  • Know RAG evaluation and MLflow. GenAI is now part of the final round.
