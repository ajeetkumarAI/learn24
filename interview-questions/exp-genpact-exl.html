02Questions and model answers

Q1
Why do you want to work in GenAI?

Answer (genuine and specific):

  • "At EXL, the analytics and operations work for insurance, banking and healthcare clients is where GenAI can automate document-heavy processes such as claims, underwriting and KYC, with clear ROI."
  • Show you understand the risks (hallucination, privacy) and how you'd manage them.
Q2
How does an LLM generate text? Explain tokens, logits and decoding.
  • The steps: 1. Text is tokenised (BPE / SentencePiece) into IDs, then embedded. 2. Decoder blocks with causal self-attention produce logits over the vocabulary for the next token. 3. Softmax turns the logits into probabilities. 4. Decoding picks a token: greedy, beam search, or sampling with temperature, top-k and top-p. 5. The new token is appended and the process repeats until a stop token or the maximum length.
  • A KV cache stores past keys and values so each new token costs O(n) attention rather than recomputing everything.
Q3
What NLP techniques would you use to extract information from insurance claim documents?
  • OCR + layout parsing for scanned forms (Document Intelligence, Textract).
  • Named-entity recognition for policy number, dates, amounts, diagnosis codes: spaCy or a fine-tuned BERT, or an LLM with a JSON schema (zero- or few-shot).
  • Classification of claim type and fraud flags; summarisation of adjuster notes.
  • Validation: regex and checksum rules, cross-checks against the policy database, and human review when confidence is low.
  • Metrics: field-level precision and recall, straight-through-processing rate, handling-time saved.
Q4
Explain dropout and batch normalisation.
  • Dropout: during training, randomly zero a fraction p of activations, so the network can't rely on specific neurons. It acts as an ensemble of sub-networks and reduces overfitting. At inference it is off (activations are scaled accordingly).
  • Batch Norm: normalises a layer's inputs per mini-batch (mean 0, variance 1), then applies a learned scale and shift. It stabilises and speeds up training and allows higher learning rates.
  • Transformers use LayerNorm instead (per token, independent of batch size), usually as pre-norm in modern LLMs.
Q5
Walk me through one project end to end.

Answer (a structure that works):

  1. Context and goal (1 line, with the metric).
  2. Data: sources, size, quality issues and the fixes.
  3. Approach: baseline → final model, and why; the alternatives you rejected.
  4. Evaluation: offline metrics plus a business check.
  5. Deployment: how it runs, how it's monitored.
  6. Impact and learnings: numbers, plus what you'd do differently. - Keep it to about 3 minutes, then let the interviewer dig in.
Q6
Python: remove duplicates from a list while preserving order.
def dedupe(xs):
    seen = set()
    return [x for x in xs if not (x in seen or seen.add(x))]

print(dedupe([3, 1, 3, 2, 1]))   # [3, 1, 2]
# or: list(dict.fromkeys(xs))   # dicts keep insertion order (Python 3.7+)
