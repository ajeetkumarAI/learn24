02Questions and model answers

Q1
How do precision and recall change as you move the classification threshold?
  • Raise the threshold: fewer positives are predicted, so precision usually rises and recall falls.
  • Lower it: more positives, so recall rises and precision falls.
  • The PR curve traces this trade-off. Choose the threshold by business cost (fraud loss vs declined good transactions) or by a target (e.g. recall ≥ 80% at the highest precision).
Q2
Why do we use log loss for classification instead of MSE?
  • Log loss = the negative log-likelihood of a Bernoulli model. It is the principled maximum-likelihood objective for probabilities.
  • With a sigmoid output it is convex in the weights (MSE + sigmoid is not), and its gradients don't vanish when the model is confidently wrong. Gradient (p − y)·x gives strong corrections.
  • It heavily penalises confident mistakes and encourages calibrated probabilities.
Q3
Derive the gradient-descent update for logistic regression.
  • Loss: L = −(1/n) Σ [y log p + (1−y) log(1−p)], with p = σ(wᵀx).
  • Using dσ/dz = σ(1−σ): ∂L/∂w = (1/n) Σ (p − y) x.
  • Update: w ← w − η · (1/n) Xᵀ(p − y). Add λw for L2.
  • Shortcomings to mention: sensitivity to the learning rate, feature scaling, and slow convergence on ill-conditioned data. Second-order methods (Newton/IRLS, L-BFGS) converge faster for small d.
Q4
Explain a customer-segmentation project end to end.
  • Goal: segment cardholders for targeted offers.
  • Features: RFM, category mix (travel, dining, groceries), channel (online/POS), cross-border share, tenure. Log-transform skewed spend, then standardise.
  • Method: k-means (choose k with the elbow, silhouette and business interpretability) or a GMM; PCA for visualisation.
  • Personas: "frequent travellers", "online shoppers", "essentials-only", "dormant".
  • Validation: stability across months; uplift from a targeted campaign A/B test (e.g. +12% activation in the travel segment).
Q5
Which unsupervised methods have you used, and for what?
  • Clustering: k-means (segments), DBSCAN (density-based, finds outliers), hierarchical (taxonomies).
  • Dimensionality reduction: PCA (compression, denoising), t-SNE/UMAP (visualisation).
  • Anomaly detection: Isolation Forest, autoencoders (reconstruction error for fraud).
  • Topic modelling / embeddings: LDA, or embeddings + clustering on complaint text.
  • Association rules: market-basket analysis.
  • For each, say why it fits and how you validated it without labels.
Q6
Design a fraud-detection model for card-not-present (online) transactions.
  • Signals: device fingerprint, IP/geo mismatch, email age, velocity per card/device/merchant, basket and amount anomalies, merchant risk, 3-D Secure result.
  • Model: gradient boosting plus graph features (shared devices/cards), with a real-time feature store; a latency budget under 50 ms.
  • Labels: chargebacks (delayed) and confirmed fraud; handle label delay and class imbalance.
  • Decision: approve / step-up (3DS/OTP) / decline, with thresholds by merchant risk.
  • Monitoring: fraud basis points, false-decline rate, drift; weekly retraining; explainability reason codes.

03How to prepare

  • Mastercard: derive losses and gradients, explain threshold effects, and present a segmentation or business case clearly.
  • Visa: timed CodeSignal practice (pandas preparation + sklearn model) and payments cases (fraud, risk, personalisation).
