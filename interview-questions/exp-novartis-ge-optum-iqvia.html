02Questions and model answers

Q1
How do you evaluate performance on each class in a multi-class problem?
  • Confusion matrix (N×N), then per-class precision, recall, F1 (one-vs-rest).
  • Aggregate: macro-average (each class counts equally, which matters for rare diseases), weighted (by support), micro (overall, equal to accuracy for single-label problems).
  • Per-class ROC-AUC / PR-AUC, plus calibration per class.
  • In medical imaging, report sensitivity and specificity per condition with confidence intervals; clinically important misses get extra weight.
Q2
Explain backpropagation.
  • Forward pass: compute the activations layer by layer and the loss.
  • Backward pass: apply the chain rule from the loss backwards. For each layer, ∂L/∂W = (∂L/∂output) × (∂output/∂W), and the gradient passes to the previous layer via ∂L/∂input.
  • Frameworks build a computation graph and do this automatically (autograd). The optimiser then updates the weights.
  • Issues: vanishing or exploding gradients, solved with good initialisation, ReLU-family activations, normalisation, residual connections and gradient clipping.
Q3
How did you design your training dataset (GE HealthCare)?
  • Representativeness: patients from several sites, scanners and vendors, demographics, disease severities. Avoid single-hospital bias.
  • Splits by patient (never by image) to prevent leakage; an external site as the test set.
  • Labels: expert annotation with an inter-rater agreement check (Cohen's κ); adjudicate disagreements.
  • Imbalance: stratify; oversample rare findings carefully; augmentation that is realistic for the modality.
  • Documentation: a datasheet covering source, inclusion criteria and known gaps, needed for regulatory submission.
Q4
IQVIA guesstimate: average daily income of an 8-lane toll plaza.

Answer (structure):

  • 8 lanes, so about 4 per direction. Peak throughput with FASTag is about 300 vehicles per lane per hour; the average over 24 hours is about 40% of peak, giving about 120 vehicles per lane per hour.
  • 8 × 120 × 24 ≈ 23,000 vehicles a day.
  • Mix: 70% cars at ₹100, 20% buses and trucks at ₹300, 10% heavy multi-axle at ₹500 → average ≈ 70 + 60 + 50 = ₹180.
  • Daily income ≈ 23,000 × ₹180 ≈ ₹41 lakh. Sanity-check against highway traffic and state your assumptions.
Q5
Explain SQL join types with a healthcare example.
  • patients(patient_id), claims(patient_id, amount):
  • INNER JOIN: patients with at least one claim.
  • LEFT JOIN: all patients, with NULL claim fields for those without claims (to find patients with no claims, add WHERE c.patient_id IS NULL).
  • RIGHT JOIN: all claims, even orphan ones (a data-quality check).
  • FULL OUTER JOIN: everything from both tables, for reconciliation.
Q6
Novartis: how would you plan a data-science project from scratch?
  1. Define: the business or clinical question, the stakeholders, the success metric, the constraints (privacy, timeline).
  2. Data: sources, access approvals, a quality assessment.
  3. Approach: baseline → model plan, with an evaluation design (including bias and fairness checks).
  4. Milestones: discovery (2 weeks), proof of concept (4 weeks), validation, deployment and monitoring. Assign owners and risks.
  5. Governance: documentation, review gates, reproducibility. - Use a simple plan (timeline + RACI) and agree checkpoints with stakeholders.
