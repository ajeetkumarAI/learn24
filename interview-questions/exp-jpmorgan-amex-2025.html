02Questions and model answers

Q1
JPMC: perform EDA on a dataset and handle the outliers. Walk through your code.
import pandas as pd, numpy as np, matplotlib.pyplot as plt

df = pd.read_csv("transactions.csv")
print(df.shape, df.dtypes, df.isna().mean().round(3), sep="\n")
print(df.describe(percentiles=[.01, .25, .5, .75, .99]).T)

col = "amount"
q1, q3 = df[col].quantile([.25, .75]); iqr = q3 - q1
lo, hi = q1 - 1.5 * iqr, q3 + 1.5 * iqr
out = (df[col] < lo) | (df[col] > hi)
print(f"outliers: {out.mean():.2%}")

fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(10, 4))
ax[0].boxplot(df[col].dropna()); ax[0].set_title("Amount (raw)")
ax[1].hist(np.log1p(df[col].clip(lower=0)), bins=50); ax[1].set_title("log(1+amount)")
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

df["amount_capped"] = df[col].clip(lo, df[col].quantile(.995))   # winsorise, keep the rows
df["is_outlier"] = out.astype(int)                              # keep the signal for fraud models
  • Say why: in banking, outliers are often the signal (fraud), so flag them rather than deleting them. Cap them for linear models, and log-transform skewed money amounts.
  • Also mention z-scores vs IQR (IQR is robust to skew), and Isolation Forest for multivariate outliers.
Q2
How do you choose a method to handle outliers?
  • First ask whether the outlier is an error or a real rare event. Data-entry errors get fixed or removed; real extremes (large transactions) are kept.
  • Options:
  • Winsorise or cap.
  • Log or Box-Cox transform.
  • Robust scaling (median/IQR).
  • Tree models, which are insensitive to outliers.
  • Robust losses (Huber).
  • A separate model for the extreme segment.
  • Multivariate outliers: Isolation Forest, LOF, Mahalanobis distance.
  • Document the decision and its effect on model metrics; regulators may ask.
Q3
How would you build a credit-card fraud detection system at a bank?
  • Real-time scoring within about 100 ms at authorisation.
  • Features:
  • Velocity (transactions in 1 h / 24 h).
  • Amount vs the user's typical spend.
  • Merchant risk, geography and travel distance.
  • Device and channel.
  • Time of day.
  • Graph links (shared cards, devices, merchants).
  • Model: gradient boosting for tabular data plus rules for known patterns; consider a sequence model for transaction history.
  • Imbalance: class weights; PR-AUC; recall at a fixed false-positive rate.
  • Decision layer: approve / step-up (OTP) / decline, with thresholds tuned on fraud loss vs customer friction.
  • Operations:
  • Labels arrive with a delay (chargebacks), so handle that.
  • Monitor drift, retrain weekly or monthly.
  • Model risk management (SR 11-7 style validation) and explainability via reason codes.
Q4
How do GenAI use cases in banking differ from other industries?
  • Common use cases:
  • Internal knowledge assistants (policies, procedures).
  • Document extraction (KYC, trade documents).
  • Call summarisation.
  • Code assistants.
  • Research summarisation for analysts.
  • Extra constraints:
  • Data residency.
  • No customer PII leaving the bank's boundary (private endpoints or self-hosted models).
  • Explainability and audit trails.
  • Model risk governance.
  • Strict accuracy on numbers.
  • Patterns: RAG with citations, a human in the loop for any customer-facing or decision output, deterministic tools for calculations (never let the LLM do the arithmetic), and red-teaming for prompt injection.
Q5
Amex: guesstimate how many transactions per second your fraud model must handle.

Answer (show the structure, not the exact number):

  • Assume about 100 million active cards, with about 1.5 transactions per card per day → about 150 million transactions per day.
  • 150M / 86,400 s ≈ 1,700 TPS on average. Peak (festive sales, evenings) is roughly 3–5× → design for about 8,000 TPS.
  • At a p99 latency budget of about 50 ms per score, with each instance handling about 500 requests per second → about 16 instances, plus headroom across 2 regions.
  • State your assumptions clearly, sanity-check them, and tie them to the design (caching features in Redis, horizontal scaling).
Q6
Explain the vanishing gradient problem and how modern networks avoid it.
  • The problem: in backpropagation through many layers, gradients are products of derivatives. With sigmoid or tanh (derivatives below 1), they shrink exponentially, so early layers barely learn. RNNs suffer this over time steps.
  • Fixes:
  • ReLU / GELU activations.
  • Residual connections (ResNets, Transformers).
  • Normalisation (Batch / Layer Norm).
  • Careful initialisation (He / Xavier).
  • LSTM / GRU gates for sequences.
  • Gradient clipping, for exploding gradients.
Q7
Behavioural (JPMC VP): how do you lead a data science team through a model that fails validation?
  • Own it: "Our credit model failed independent validation on stability (PSI 0.3 in one segment)."
  • Act: 1. Run a root-cause review with the team, without blame. 2. Find the issue: a feature driven by a policy change. 3. Rebuild with stable features and add segment-level monitoring. 4. Communicate the timeline to the business and risk teams every week.
  • Result: "It passed revalidation in five weeks. We added stability tests to our standard development checklist, and the next two models passed on the first submission."
  • A VP-level answer shows governance, communication, coaching and process improvement.

03How to prepare for banking AI roles

  • Hands-on first: practise timed EDA, outlier handling and plotting in pandas, NumPy and matplotlib.
  • Know fraud, credit risk, imbalance, calibration and model risk management basics.
  • For GenAI roles, emphasise privacy, governance, citations and human-in-the-loop design.
  • Practise guesstimates on your own projects (users, TPS, cost).
