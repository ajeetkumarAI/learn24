02Questions and model answers

Q1
ServiceNow coding: choose stocks to maximise profit within a budget (each stock has a cost and an expected profit).
def max_profit(costs, profits, budget):          # 0/1 knapsack
    dp = [0] * (budget + 1)
    for c, p in zip(costs, profits):
        for b in range(budget, c - 1, -1):
            dp[b] = max(dp[b], dp[b - c] + p)
    return dp[budget]
print(max_profit([5, 4, 6, 3], [10, 40, 30, 50], 10))   # 90
  • This is 0/1 knapsack: O(n·B) time, O(B) space. If the problem is instead "buy and sell one stock over time", it is a single pass tracking the minimum price.
  • Clarify assumptions first (integer costs? one share each?). The candidate's frustration came from mismatched assumptions.
Q2
How would you build a GenAI assistant that resolves IT tickets automatically?
  • Inputs: the ticket text, the user's role, CMDB (asset) data, past resolved tickets, the knowledge base.
  • Flow: 1. Classify the category and priority. 2. Retrieve similar resolved tickets and KB articles (hybrid search). 3. Draft a resolution or a suggested action. 4. For safe automations (password reset, access request), call workflow tools with approval policies.
  • Guardrails: role-based tool permissions, a human agent in the loop for changes, an audit trail.
  • Metrics: deflection rate, mean time to resolve, reopen rate, agent acceptance of suggestions.
Q3
SAP: explain the types of machine learning with business examples.
  • Supervised: predict labelled outcomes, such as invoice approval (classification) or demand forecasting (regression).
  • Unsupervised: find structure, such as vendor clustering or anomaly detection in journal entries.
  • Reinforcement: learn by reward, such as inventory replenishment policies or dynamic pricing.
  • Self-supervised / foundation models: LLMs for document understanding, a copilot over ERP data.
Q4
How do you ground an LLM copilot on ERP data safely?
  • Structured data: use tools/APIs (OData services) or text-to-SQL over a curated semantic layer, rather than dumping tables into prompts.
  • Unstructured data: RAG over policies and documentation.
  • Authorisation: enforce the user's existing business roles on every tool call; never give the model broader access than the user has.
  • Accuracy: calculations go through deterministic services; answers cite the source records; confirmation before posting transactions.
  • Compliance: data residency, audit logs, no customer data used for training without consent.
Q5
Walk through your project on a screen share. How do you make it effective?
  • Open with a 30-second summary (problem, users, result), then show the architecture diagram, then a live demo of the core flow, then the code for one interesting part (for example, the retrieval logic).
  • Have a fallback (screenshots or video) if the demo fails. Know your repo structure well enough to navigate instantly.
Q6
What do you know about S/4HANA, and why might that matter for an AI role at SAP?
  • S/4HANA is SAP's ERP suite on the in-memory HANA database, covering finance, supply chain, procurement and manufacturing.
  • For AI: it is the system of record that copilots and predictions act on (cash-flow forecasting, invoice matching, supply-risk prediction). Understanding business processes (order-to-cash, procure-to-pay) lets you build AI that fits real workflows.

  • SAP: a built AI/RAG project you can demo, ML basics, and basic knowledge of SAP products and processes.
