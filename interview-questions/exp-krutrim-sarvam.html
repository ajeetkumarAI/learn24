02Questions and model answers

Q1
Why do Indic languages need special handling in LLMs?
  • Tokenisation: English-centric BPE splits Devanagari and Tamil text into many tokens, which raises cost and latency and lowers quality. Indic-aware tokenisers (larger vocabularies, script-specific merges) cut the token count 2–4×.
  • Data scarcity and quality: less web text, so use translation-augmented, synthetic and curated data, and careful deduplication.
  • Code-mixing and transliteration: Hinglish in Latin script ("kal milte hain"). Train on romanised text too.
  • Evaluation: benchmarks such as IndicGLUE, IndicXTREME, FLORES for translation, and human evaluation by native speakers.
Q2
How would you evaluate a speech-to-speech dubbing pipeline (ASR → translation → TTS)?
  • Per component:
  • ASR: WER/CER.
  • Translation: BLEU/chrF/COMET plus human adequacy and fluency.
  • TTS: MOS, intelligibility (ASR on the TTS output), speaker-similarity cosine of voice embeddings.
  • End to end:
  • Lip-sync and timing alignment.
  • Preservation of emotion and prosody.
  • Naturalness ratings.
  • Error propagation analysis.
  • Non-determinism: fix seeds, sample several outputs, report distributions; use A/B preference tests against global baselines.
Q3
How do you optimise LLM inference on GPUs?
  • Serving engine: vLLM / TensorRT-LLM / SGLang with continuous batching, a paged KV cache, prefix caching.
  • Model: FP8/INT8/INT4 quantization (AWQ, GPTQ), GQA, speculative decoding (a small draft model), distillation.
  • Kernels: FlashAttention, fused ops, CUDA graphs.
  • Measure: time to first token, tokens per second per GPU, p99 latency, cost per 1M tokens; profile with Nsight. Tune batch size against latency targets.
Q4
Tell us about your previous work in depth (Krutrim technical round).

Answer (structure for research or engineering work):

  1. Problem and why it matters (one sentence).
  2. What existed before and its limitation.
  3. Your approach: the key idea, the architecture, the data, why it should work.
  4. Experiments: baselines, ablations, metrics, negative results.
  5. What you'd do next. - Research scientists will dig into ablations and failure cases, so be honest about what didn't work.
Q5
Why do you want to join an Indian AI lab rather than a big tech company?
  • "I want to build models for a billion users in their own languages, problems global labs under-serve: Indic speech, low-resource translation, voice-first interfaces."
  • "Smaller teams mean ownership across data, training and deployment."
  • Mention specific products (Sarvam's Indic models and voice stack; Krutrim's multilingual models and cloud) to show genuine interest.
Q6
How would you build an Indic-language RAG assistant for government-scheme questions?
  • Corpus: scheme PDFs in several languages → OCR as needed → chunk by section → multilingual embeddings (e.g. BGE-M3, LaBSE-style).
  • Query: speech input (ASR) in the user's language → retrieve across languages → answer in the user's language with an Indic-capable LLM → optional TTS.
  • Guardrails: cite the scheme clause; "I don't know" with a helpline fallback; simple vocabulary.
  • Evaluation: questions written by native speakers per language; faithfulness plus comprehension testing with real users.

03How to prepare

  • Know your own projects deeply: ablations, failures, numbers.
  • Learn Indic NLP and speech basics (tokenisation, ASR/TTS metrics), and LLM inference optimisation.
